# General Schematic Design Guidelines (Sample)

> Narrative guidance used to give retrieval-augmented agents extra grounded context
> beyond raw numeric rules. Synthetic, for the semester proof-of-concept only.

## Site Planning
Favor placing the building mass toward the street frontage to activate the public edge
and push surface parking to the side or rear of the site where it is less visible from
the street, provided fire access and accessible routes are preserved. Where the
required parking count cannot reasonably fit on-site as surface parking within the
buildable envelope, the workflow should say so explicitly and recommend structured or
podium parking as a Phase 2 design decision rather than silently under-providing
parking or overrunning setbacks.

## Building Layout
A double-loaded corridor typology is the default BaaP-efficient layout: two rows of
unit modules with a single corridor and a shared core. Corners and ends of a floor
plate are good locations for larger 2-bedroom modules, since they benefit from dual
exposure. Accessible units should be placed near the core, not at the far end of a long
corridor.

## What "Insufficient Information" Looks Like
The workflow should decline to assert a final compliant design (returning
"insufficient information" or "requires professional review") when: the customer
program's hard requirements cannot geometrically fit the buildable envelope even after
reasonable BaaP substitution; the applicable zoning source is missing or unconfirmed;
or required site information (survey, easements, floodplain) is absent. It is better to
surface a clearly-flagged gap than to fabricate a plausible-looking but ungrounded
number.

## Reporting Expectations
A useful schematic-stage output pairs a drawing (site plan / floor plate) with a short
written compliance narrative: what was assumed, what is confirmed vs. unconfirmed,
what conflicts were found, and what a professional reviewer should check first.
